# Navigation

`SideNav` is a pure structural component: it decides "flat top item →
grouped middle → de-emphasised footer" and the treatment per breakpoint.
Routing, session data and the current path all come from the caller — it
never hardcodes a route and has no dependency on any router.

What makes it worth a page of its own is the responsive behaviour, which
is not a set of widths but a single rule with one exception.

Every story below is linked rather than embedded, and that is not
laziness: the rails are `fixed` and portalled to `document.body`, so a
story canvas rendered inline in this page would attach them to *this*
page and float them over the prose. Each band also needs a pinned
viewport, which only the story canvas provides — `SideNav`'s stories
carry named viewports for exactly that, because a breakpoint story that
cannot show its own breakpoint is where responsive bugs hide.

## The rule: only the sidebar takes space out of the page

Every band except the widest one **floats over** the content. That is
what lets a caller build one layout instead of one layout per band.

`collapsed` is a JS boolean rather than a breakpoint, because it is a
*preference* rather than a measurement — the caller owns it, persists it,
and usually puts a toggle beside it. Note what it means: not "the
sidebar, narrower", but the sidebar ceasing to exist and the rail taking
over. That is the only version that actually gives the content its width
back.

Everything else is plain CSS — `sm:` / `xl:` Tailwind variants, no
`useMediaQuery`, no viewport read. Two parallel trees, each visible only
in its own band, rather than one tree whose content changes with a
JS-read width. The cost is one extra copy of the links in the markup, and
`display: none` removes a subtree from the accessibility tree entirely,
so a screen reader still reaches each destination exactly once.

**`toolbarVariant`** picks M3's two schemes. `"standard"` (the default) is a
`surface-2` bar with muted icons and the current page as a filled
`primary` pill. `"vibrant"` is a `primary` bar — the inverse of the page,
near-white on dark and near-black on light — with the current page cut
back out of it in `surface-2`. Neither is a hue: §1.3's one-accent rule
still holds, and a tinted bar would read as a status.

**No shadow, exactly as androidx.** Compose ships this toolbar at
elevation `Level0`, and so does this one — no border, no blur, no
shadow; the fill is the only separation. The cost is stated rather than
hidden: the bar floats over whatever a consumer's page scrolls under it,
and on the dark theme `surface-2` over a `surface-2` card is the same
colour, so over such a card the standard bar's edge disappears and only
its icons remain. A soft shadow was weighed against that and dropped in
favour of M3's `Level0`. A screen that puts `surface-2` content under
the bar should use `toolbarVariant="vibrant"`, whose contrast with the
page does not depend on what scrolls underneath.

## Why the in-flow icon rail was removed

There used to be a fourth band: an in-flow 64px icon rail from
1024–1279px. Its removal is the fix, not a simplification.

**The bug:** at exactly 1024px the floating rail vanished and a
full-height strip appeared *below* the page content. `SideNav` stopped
being `fixed` at `lg` and became an in-flow flex child, so it landed
wherever it happened to sit in the caller's DOM — which, in a layout
built for a rail that floats, is "after everything".

The reason no caller could have avoided that is the general point:
**it was the only shape that needed a flex-row parent while its
neighbours needed none.** A caller had to build one layout for the
floating rail and another for the icon rail and get the crossover exactly
right. No single caller layout could satisfy both.

The band survives in `smallScreen="off-canvas"`, where every shape is in
flow and the contradiction does not arise.

[**Story: Full sidebar**](?path=/story/primitives-sidenav--full-sidebar) — the ≥1280px band, pinned at 1440px. [**Collapsed on desktop**](?path=/story/primitives-sidenav--collapsed-on-desktop) is the same width with the rail taking over.

## Below 640px the rail turns rather than shrinks

A 64px column is 17% of a 375px viewport, permanently, down the side the
writing starts on — and it sits where a thumb cannot comfortably reach on
a phone held one-handed. Both problems are about the **axis**, not the
width. Along the bottom the rail costs height in the region every mobile
OS already reserves for chrome, and it lands under the thumb.

[**Story: Tiny-screen rail**](?path=/story/primitives-sidenav--tiny-screen-rail) — pinned at 375px, with the current page deliberately behind the overflow menu.

**Four slots**, and the number is a width measurement rather than a taste
one: the current page's 64px pill, four 48px targets (three slots plus
the menu button), four 4px gaps and the toolbar's own 16px of padding
come to 288px. At 375px — the narrowest phone this library targets —
that leaves the toolbar about 43px clear of each edge. Five slots would
be 340px, leaving 17px — barely more than M3's 16px screen offset, so it
reads as a docked bar that lost its corners — and six only fits by
shrinking the targets below 48px, which is the one dimension not
available to trade.

Every toolbar item carries an explicit size, and that is a lesson kept.
An earlier revision let a horizontal rail's anchors take their width
from their parent; in a row with `items-center` there was nothing to
stretch to, and they collapsed to a **16×32px** tap target, measured, on
the one form factor where that is least acceptable.
`e2e/floating-toolbar.spec.ts` now pins every horizontal item's target
at 48px tall and 48px wide (64px for the current page), and
`e2e/geometry.spec.ts` holds both toolbars' links to their floors.

### The overflow menu's rows are anchors

Everything past the fourth slot — plus **all** footer items, which are
administrivia and lose to any destination for a slot — goes behind one
menu control. A sideways-scrolling strip of icons with no scrollbar and
no affordance is the pattern where destinations simply go unfound, and it
is also the exact class of unwanted horizontal scroll this library has
spent two rounds removing. A menu is the honest version: the rest sits
*visibly* behind one control rather than invisibly past an edge.

Those rows use **`DropdownMenuLinkItem`, not `DropdownMenuItem`**, and
the distinction is not cosmetic. `DropdownMenuItem` renders a `<button>`;
`DropdownMenuLinkItem` renders an `<a href>`. A destination behind a menu
must still support middle-click, cmd-click and "copy link address", or
the overflow items become second-class links purely because of where they
landed in the order. The item type was added for this component.

If the current page is one of the hidden ones, the menu button becomes
the current page's filled pill — otherwise the toolbar shows nothing as
current, which reads as a bug.

[**Story: Floating rail overflow**](?path=/story/primitives-sidenav--floating-rail-overflow) — more destinations than slots.

## The portal, and what it costs

Both rails are rendered through `createPortal` to `document.body`.

The reason is specific. A `fixed` element's containing block is the
viewport **only** while no ancestor establishes one — and `transform`,
`filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `contain` and `will-change: transform` all
do. That is not hypothetical: this package's own `Drawer` is exactly such
an ancestor, because `vaul` stamps
`[data-vaul-drawer]{will-change:transform}` unconditionally, with no
state gate. Read it in `vaul/dist/index.mjs`; it is still true. Every
consumer that wraps `SideNav` in a drawer would have re-anchored the rail
to the drawer's box instead of the viewport.

A portalled node's containing block resolves against the document root,
so no ancestor of the caller's can capture it. `side-nav.portal.test.tsx`
pins that directly by rendering inside a `transform: translateZ(0)`
wrapper and asserting both rails' `parentElement` is `document.body` —
not "somewhere outside", but specifically body, the only ancestor chain
guaranteed free of a transform a consumer introduced.

### The SSR cost, stated plainly

`createPortal` needs a real DOM node, and `document` does not exist while
this component renders on the server. So `FloatingRailPortal` renders
`null` until an effect confirms a client has mounted it — `useState` plus
`useEffect`, because the question is not "what breakpoint are we at"
(still answered by CSS everywhere else) but "does `document.body` exist
yet".

The consequence: in the default `"floating"` mode there is **no
small-screen navigation at all** in the server-rendered HTML, and none
for a client whose JS has not finished hydrating or has failed to load.
Every other band — the sidebar, and the whole off-canvas tree — renders
with no JS at all. Verify that rather than trusting it: `curl` a page and
grep the raw HTML for a nav item's label.

This is a narrower version of a gap this component was rewritten to
eliminate. An earlier revision used a `useMediaQuery` hook and wrapped
`SideNav` in `next/dynamic({ ssr: false })` to work around its SSR crash,
which meant the sidebar never appeared in server HTML at all and every
full page load painted an empty slab. The remaining gap is scoped to one
band, in one mode, and is bounded by hydration rather than by a lazy
chunk's network round trip. It is not zero.

**A caller who cannot accept any gap below `lg`** — a bot that only sees
first-paint HTML, or a strict no-JS requirement — should use
`smallScreen="off-canvas"`, which has none.

## `smallScreen="off-canvas"`

The opt-in mode: the original full-label accordion tree, groups
collapsible via a Headless UI `Disclosure` and expanded only if they
contain the current route, for a caller who already owns a drawer and
wants this component to fill it rather than float over it.

It differs from the default in four ways worth knowing:

- Every band is in flow, including the 1024–1279px icon rail.
- No rails, no portal, no mount gate: it is fully server-rendered.
- `accountSlot` works below `lg`. In `"floating"` mode it never renders
  below `lg` at all, because 64px has no room for an email address and
  a sign-out control, and there is no wider sibling column to defer to.
- `collapsed` means nothing, because the mode has its own in-flow tree at
  every width.

Note the upgrade hazard if you already used this component: `"floating"`
is now the **default**. A consumer who previously rendered `SideNav`
inside their own drawer and passed nothing now gets both — their drawer
(still openable, still empty, exactly as before) *and* a floating rail
permanently on screen underneath it. That consumer passes
`smallScreen="off-canvas"` explicitly to get their single below-`lg`
experience back.

[**Story: Off-canvas drawer**](?path=/story/primitives-sidenav--off-canvas-drawer) — the accordion tree inside a 300px stand-in for a caller's drawer.

## What the caller still owns

**The content column's padding.** A `fixed` rail cannot reserve its own
space — that is what `fixed` means — so nothing this component renders
can push the page over. The caller adds the inset, and it has to change
with the band, because which side the rail is on changes at 640px: below
that it is along the bottom, and a left gutter would be reserving space
for nothing. The stories use `pb-24 sm:pb-6 sm:pl-24 xl:pl-6` on the
content column for exactly this: the bottom toolbar ends 80px above the
bottom edge (64px tall, 16px off it) and the vertical one 80px from the
left (16px in, 64px wide), so 96px leaves a 16px gutter either way.

**This moved with the M3 toolbar.** The rail used to be a 52px pill
12px in, ending at 64px, and `sm:pl-20` was right for it. The toolbar
now ends at exactly 80px, so a caller still on `sm:pl-20` gets content
touching its edge — move to `sm:pl-24`.

**The current path.** A plain string, resolved by the caller's own
router.

**The account block's markup.** Rendered as-is through `accountSlot`, so
this component stays free of any auth-specific knowledge.

**The collapse preference**, if the application offers one.

## Labels in the rail are a native `title`

Every rail row is icon-only. Its accessible name comes from an `sr-only`
span; the *visual* label on hover is a native `title` on an `aria-hidden`
wrapper, so it stays purely visual and is not announced a second time as
a description.

That is a correction rather than a preference, and it was measured on a
real render at 1100px before anything changed. daisyUI's `.tooltip`
renders its bubble as an absolutely positioned pseudo-element at
`left: 100%`, and the `<nav>` is a scroll container:

```text
nav.clientWidth   40      // the rail, content-sized
nav.scrollWidth   212     // 172px of it unreachable
::before content  "Dashboard"
::before left     32px
::before width    82.6px  // i.e. 32→115px, entirely outside the clip box
```

The bubble was clipped away on every hover — no label ever appeared in
the icon rail — while still counting toward the scrollable area, which
was the stray horizontal scrollbar under the rail. One cause, two
symptoms.

There is no CSS-only way out: any flyout anchored inside a scroll
container is clipped by it, and escaping needs either a portal or CSS
anchor positioning. A native `title` is painted by the browser *outside*
the page entirely, so nothing can clip it, and it costs no CSS and no JS.
That is also why the vertical rail stays usable once it outgrows
`max-h-[80vh]` and starts scrolling.

Labelled rows get no `title`. The label is already on screen, and a
native tooltip repeating text the reader can see is noise on every row to
spare the rare one that is actually cut.

## One landmark is ever displayed, and three carry the label

All three shapes are `<nav aria-label="Primary">`. The rails were plain
`<div>`s until the a11y gate pointed out that below 640px the in-flow
`<nav>` is `display: none` — so a phone got its navigation outside any
landmark at all.

Three identically-named landmarks in the DOM is only safe because exactly
one is ever displayed, and their breakpoint gates are complements. jsdom
applies no CSS, so axe sees all three and reports `landmark-unique`,
which here genuinely is a false positive. That one rule is disabled in
that one block with the reason written down, and the invariant it would
have checked is pinned instead on the breakpoint gates themselves in
`side-nav.portal.test.tsx` — the weaker kind of test, pinning the
mechanism rather than the result, chosen because the result is not
observable without a real layout and the alternative is pinning nothing.

The rails carry a `data-floating-rail` attribute. It is a stable hook
rather than a test-only wart: the subtree is portalled, so it is the one
part of `SideNav` a caller cannot reach through the element they
rendered, and both the portal test and a consumer's
`body > [data-floating-rail]` override need it. Selecting on layout
classes instead would make a styling change silently break both.
