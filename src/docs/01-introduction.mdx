# @vaam-apps/ui

A dark-first React component library for **operator consoles** — the screens a
person uses to find out why one record out of a hundred thousand did not do
what it was supposed to.

That sentence is the whole brief, and most of what follows is a consequence of
it. An operator console is not a marketing surface and it is not a
general-purpose admin CRUD scaffold. Its reader is scanning a column of a
thousand rows looking for the four that are wrong, then opening one of them and
reading every field on it carefully. Those two activities want different
surfaces, and the library says so out loud rather than leaving it to taste.

Styling is **daisyUI**, so component classes stay short and a consumer can
theme them without forking. Behaviour is **Headless UI**, so focus management,
keyboard handling and ARIA are not reimplemented once per component. State
machines are rendered through a **parameterised status system**, so one glyph
vocabulary serves every enum in an application instead of one badge component
per enum.

## Colour means something here

This is the premise the rest of the system is built on, and it is worth stating
before anything else, because it is the reason several otherwise reasonable
decisions were refused.

Eight hues exist, and each one answers a question about a record: is it
finished, did it work, and who is holding it. `neutral`, `progress` and
`parked` are the three ways a record can be unfinished — nobody has it, this
system has it, somebody outside has it. `success`, `danger` and `expired` are
the three ways it can be over. `warning` and `uncertain` are the two ways it
can want a human, and they are not the same want: `warning` means somebody can
act and acting will help, `uncertain` means the outcome will never be learned.
The README's hue and mark tables are the working reference for choosing between
them.

Colour is never the only channel. Each state also differs in silhouette,
interior mark and fill, because colour alone fails for the roughly 8% of men
with a colour-vision deficiency and fails completely in a monochrome screenshot
pasted into a ticket. The redundancy is load-bearing rather than polite: inside
a 14px glyph, the mark is doing much of the work of telling the closest two
hues apart.

The corollary is the part people trip over. **If a hue means a state, then a
coloured surface anywhere else is a lie.** A tinted card, a per-state border on
a panel, a chrome accent picked because it looked good — each of them invites a
reader to infer a status that nobody encoded. So there is no per-state tinting
of chrome in this library, and there is exactly one coloured surface: the
`--aurora-*` ramp behind `InstrumentPanel` and `Card glow`. It is bound by
`color-mix` to the system's own hues so it cannot drift into a fifth invented
palette, and it carries **no meaning at all** — no status is inferable from it,
there is no per-state variant, and a caller cannot tint it. That absence of
meaning, not a separate hue, is the only reason a coloured surface is safe here.

## Three surface registers

`InstrumentPanel`'s module doc names the map, and it is the fastest orientation
into the library's layout decisions. The registers are distinguished by what
the reader is *doing*, not by what the component is called.

The three panels below are the real components, rendered on this page. They sit
inside their own `data-theme="dark"` wrapper, because a documentation page is
not a story and the preview decorator that normally stamps that attribute on
`<html>` never runs here — without it, every custom property declared under
`[data-theme="dark"]` resolves to nothing. That is the same gap
[Installation](?path=/docs/docs-installation--docs) describes for an
attribute-less application page.

- **Diagnostic** — tables, drawers, detail panes. You *read* these, looking for
  the one row that is wrong. A hairline on a surface step, and nothing else
  competing for attention. This is most of the library.
- **Floating** — popover, dialog, drawer, toast. These overlap a ground they do
  not know about, so they get a shadow. Two of them, `--shadow-popover` and
  `--shadow-dialog`, and the light theme redefines both because a near-black
  shadow on a light ground reads as dirt rather than as depth.
- **Instrument** — dashboards and metric rows. You *scan* these, and the job is
  to make one number findable in a glance. An aurora mesh ground, no border:
  a hairline on top of the mesh reads as two competing boundaries.

The third register is an exception with a budget, not a loosening of the rule.
A glow on every card is a glow on none, and borders remain the default
everywhere the rule was doing work. `InstrumentPanel`'s own mesh is capped at
14% alpha and the panel steps its caption tier up from `subtle` to `muted`,
because a gradient ground breaks the assumption every contrast check in the
system rests on — see [Theming and tokens](?path=/docs/docs-theming-and-tokens--docs).

## What is deliberately not here

A library is as much what it refuses. Each of these is a decision with a
written reason in the source, not a gap waiting to be filled, and reopening one
should mean arguing with the reason rather than noticing the absence.

- **No rich or interactive tooltips.** `Tooltip` is daisyUI's CSS
  `.tooltip`/`data-tip` — no JS, no portal, hover and focus driven by
  `:hover`/`:focus` alone. `data-tip` is rendered through
  `content: attr(data-tip)`, so a label must be a plain string. Headless UI
  ships no tooltip, and adding a second behaviour library to replace one
  positioning dependency with another was the trade being declined. The
  component also sets a native `title` alongside, as a fallback for the case a
  scrolling ancestor clips the positioned bubble away.
- **No per-state tinting of chrome.** See above. The aurora ramp is the one
  coloured surface, and its whole safety argument is that it means nothing.
- **No success or warning button.** Those hues belong to status. A button is
  primary, secondary, ghost or destructive. Mixing the two vocabularies is how
  a status language erodes: once a green button exists, green has two meanings.
- **No `info` hue, and blue is selection only.** `--ring` is focus and
  selection, never a state. `theme.css` even pins daisyUI's own
  `--color-info` to the neutral grey with a written reason. The two states an
  `info` hue is usually reached for are `progress` and `parked`.
- **No syntax-highlighting palette in `PayloadInspector`.** A rainbow JSON
  block would be the loudest thing on a diagnostic screen, on a surface whose
  entire job is to be read carefully.
- **No centred empty-state placards.** Empty states are inline status lines —
  `InlineEmptyState` — with no illustration and no card. The one exception is
  `variant="standalone"`, a single line plus one action on a screen with
  nothing else to do.
- **No skeleton shimmer.** A shimmer sweeps on a fixed period, which reads as
  progress a placeholder cannot know about, and a column of them beats like a
  metronome. `Skeleton` runs a chaotic aurora drift on coprime periods (23s and
  31s) instead, and stops entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **No toast for anything an operator must act on.** Toasts are for transient
  confirmations — copied, saved, replay queued. A toast that expires while
  somebody is reading a payload is a lost message, so anything actionable is
  inline.
- **No font files.** Four family stacks are named; none of them are
  `@font-face` rules. See [Installation](?path=/docs/docs-installation--docs).
- **No authorisation inferred from presentation.** A status system's `family`
  field is metadata for "should this row stop polling", and nothing else. A UI
  that greys out *cancel* because its own table says `terminal` will be wrong
  the first time the state machine changes and nobody remembers the table
  exists. Propose the action; let the server refuse it.
